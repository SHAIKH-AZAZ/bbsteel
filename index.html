From the drawing to the cutting yard

  1. Upload the drawing

    DWG, DXF, PDF, Revit or an Excel schedule, filed under the project, block and floor it belongs to.

  2. Check what was read

    BBSteel pulls out every member schedule: beam sizes and bars, column rings, slab and footing mats. You review and correct it before anything is calculated.

  3. Calculate every bar

    Lap length from your project's coefficient times bar diameter, development length, cover, bends and stirrup zones. Bar shapes come from your own shape library.

  4. Export the schedule

    A BBS in Excel with bar code, dia, total bars, cutting length and weight per diameter from 8 to 40 mm, plus a waste report that packs every cut into stock bars.

AI that reads structural drawings

Schedules come in dozens of layouts, from neat tables to bar marks scattered beside a section. BBSteel first works out which layout a sheet uses, then reads it with instructions written for that layout, and checks itself where it's unsure.

  1. Render

    Each PDF page is rendered at up to 700 DPI, so small bar marks stay legible.

  2. Recognise the layout

    A vision classifier matches the sheet to one of nearly 50 known schedule layouts across beams, columns, slabs and footings.

  3. Find the schedule

    The schedule region is cropped and sliced, so nothing is read at thumbnail size.

  4. Extract

    A vision model reads member marks, sizes, main bars and stirrups, and zooms back in to re-read anything it isn't sure of.

  5. Tidy up

    Grouped marks like C1, C2 are split into separate members, values are normalised and grouped by floor, ready for your review.

Cutting-stock optimisation, built in-house

Each bar diameter is a cutting-stock problem: fit every cut length into as few stock bars as possible. BBSteel runs its own solvers on each one and keeps the best plan.

Greedy

Places the longest cuts first. Fast, and the floor every other plan has to beat.

Dynamic programming

Searches for cutting patterns that use a stock bar as fully as possible.

Branch and bound

Explores combinations of patterns and drops the ones that can't win.

Swap improvement

Starts from the greedy plan and moves pieces between bars to cut waste further.

The optimiser runs every solver the job size allows on the same cuts and keeps the best plan, so the result is never worse than greedy. It runs in a background worker pool, so a large project doesn't hold up the app.

What it reads

Beams

Single and continuous beams. Continuous top bars are walked along the stock length with laps placed in the lap zone and never inside a column, staggered where you ask for it.

Stirrups can change spacing or diameter across the left, middle and right of each span.

Columns

Main bars per floor, with the confinement zone taken from the next floor's height. Ring counts are worked out separately for the bottom, middle and top zones.

Slabs

Bar lengths and counts from the slab schedule, using the formulas set on each bar shape.

Footings

Flat, stepped and stirruped footings, read from the footing schedule.
